Transfer learning and convolutional neural networks for solar photovoltaic systems detection in distributed power grids

Y.H. Chou†, V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, (2026). Transfer learning and convolutional neural networks for solar photovoltaic systems detection in distributed power grids, Renewable Energy (accepted, in editorial production).

Mapping distributed solar installations is a prerequisite for understanding their contribution to the grid. This work uses convolutional neural networks and transfer learning to detect photovoltaic panels in remotely sensed imagery. Pretrained models provide reusable image features, while hard example mining focuses training on difficult cases. The approach supports more scalable inventories of distributed generation for energy planning and net-demand assessment.

Routing Optimization Framework for Exploring Time-Varying Urban Road Network Vulnerability under Floods

V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, A. Sankarasubramanian, R. Ranjithan, (2026). Routing Optimization Framework for Exploring Time-Varying Urban Road Network Vulnerability under Floods, Journal of Infrastructure Systems.

How does a usable route change as a hurricane floods a city? This study combines street-level inundation estimates with routing optimization, representing both road closures and slower travel through flooded streets. Case studies of Hurricanes Florence, Matthew, and Dorian in Wilmington, North Carolina, show why storm-specific flood estimates reveal changing bottlenecks and vulnerable areas that static flood maps can miss.

Fused Portfolio Optimization for Harnessing Marine Renewable Energy Resources

M. Maceda, R. Miller†, V.A.D. de Faria, M. Bryant, C. Vermillion, A.R. de Queiroz, (2026). Fused Portfolio Optimization for Harnessing Marine Renewable Energy Resources, Energy.

This framework chooses where to deploy offshore energy devices while also optimizing their design, size, and technology mix. It maximizes power delivered to shore under an electricity-cost constraint, accounting for resource conditions and transmission limits. A North Carolina case study combining wind turbines and marine hydrokinetic kites demonstrates how coordinating device design with portfolio decisions can improve the use of shared transmission infrastructure.

Optimizing co-located offshore wave and wind energy systems: Feasibility, design, and portfolios assessment

N. Jamaleddin, V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, M. Gabr, (2025). Optimizing co-located offshore wave and wind energy systems: Feasibility, design, and portfolios assessment, Journal of Cleaner Production.

Can wind turbines and wave-energy devices share offshore infrastructure effectively? This study combines portfolio optimization with dynamic mooring simulations to assess technology mixes, transmission limits, curtailment, and shared anchoring. Cases along the U.S. East Coast examine Pelamis and RM3 wave converters alongside wind turbines. The analysis identifies opportunities to use transmission capacity more fully, while showing that wave-energy costs remain a barrier to deployment.

Modeling energy storage in long-term capacity expansion energy planning: an analysis of the Italian system

M. Nicoli, V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, L. Savoldi, (2024). Modeling energy storage in long-term capacity expansion energy planning: an analysis of the Italian system, Journal of Energy Storage.

Long-term investment models need to capture the daily operating patterns that make storage valuable. This study adds more detailed representations of renewable availability, demand, and storage operation to TEMOA and applies them to Italy. Comparisons across time resolutions and technology choices show that storage plays a central role in full power-sector decarbonization by 2050 when low-carbon baseload generation is unavailable.

Bayesian modeling and mechanical simulations for fragility curve estimation of the mooring system of marine hydrokinetic devices

V.A.D. de Faria, N. Jamaleddin, A.R. de Queiroz, M. Gabr, (2024). Bayesian modeling and mechanical simulations for fragility curve estimation of the mooring system of marine hydrokinetic devices, Applied Ocean Research.

This study estimates how hurricane conditions translate into damage risk for the moorings of ocean-current energy devices. Bayesian modeling combines with ANSYS AQWA mechanical simulations and current measurements off North Carolina to construct fragility curves linking wind speed to failure probability. The results show that variations in current velocity, and the dynamic loads they create, matter alongside average current speed when assessing device vulnerability.

Scenario Generation and Risk-averse Stochastic Portfolio Optimization Applied to Offshore Renewable Energy Technologies

V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, J.F. DeCarolis, (2023). Scenario Generation and Risk-averse Stochastic Portfolio Optimization Applied to Offshore Renewable Energy Technologies, Energy.

A generative adversarial neural network creates synthetic wind, wave, and ocean-current scenarios that preserve patterns observed across more than 500 offshore sites. A stochastic optimization model then selects device locations and quantities while limiting electricity cost and improving generation in the worst five percent of conditions. The resulting portfolios illustrate how diversifying offshore technologies can reduce exposure to low-output periods.

An Overview of Short-Term Load Forecasting for Electricity Systems Operational Planning: Machine Learning Methods and the Brazilian Experience

G. Aquila, L.B.S. Morais, V.A.D. de Faria, J.W.M. Lima, L.M.M. Lima, A.R. de Queiroz, (2023). An Overview of Short-Term Load Forecasting for Electricity Systems Operational Planning: Machine Learning Methods and the Brazilian Experience, Energies.

Accurate short-term demand forecasts support dispatch, reserve decisions, and electricity pricing. This review examines machine learning approaches, including neural networks and support vector machines, alongside choices about input variables, data segmentation, hierarchical forecasting, and net demand. It connects these methods to the Brazilian system operator’s day-ahead planning process and discusses how changing generation patterns increase the need for reliable forecasts.

Short-term load forecasting using neural networks and global climate models: An application to a large-scale electrical power system

L.B.S. Morais, G. Aquila, V.A.D. de Faria, L.M.M. Lima, J.W.M. Lima, A.R. de Queiroz, (2023). Short-term load forecasting using neural networks and global climate models: An application to a large-scale electrical power system, Applied Energy.

This study compares feedforward and recurrent neural networks for short-term electricity-demand forecasting, using global climate model information as additional input. Tests on Brazil’s interconnected power system benchmark predictions against the system operator’s forecasts. Bidirectional LSTM and GRU models generally perform best; the recurrent models achieve a reported mean absolute percentage error of 1.18%, compared with 2.01% for the operator’s models.

Optimizing Offshore Renewable Portfolios Under Resource Variability

V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, J.F. DeCarolis, (2022). Optimizing Offshore Renewable Portfolios Under Resource Variability, Applied Energy.

A mean–variance optimization model selects offshore wind, wave, and ocean-current sites while balancing electricity cost and generation variability. A computational relaxation allows the analysis to include more than 5,000 candidate locations off North Carolina. The selected portfolios are then evaluated in a capacity expansion model: combining resources reduces output variability, but substantial technology cost reductions are still needed for competitive deployment in the studied region.

An assessment of multi-layer perceptron networks for streamflow forecasting in large-scale interconnected hydrosystems

V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, L.M.M. Lima, J.W.M. Lima, B.C. Silva, (2022). An assessment of multi-layer perceptron networks for streamflow forecasting in large-scale interconnected hydrosystems, International Journal of Environmental Science and Technology.

This work develops short-term streamflow forecasts for 55 hydropower plants in Brazil’s Paraná Basin. It compares neural-network training methods and proposes an algorithm for selecting inputs across a large interconnected river system, including historical rainfall information. The resulting multilayer perceptron models generally outperform the hydrological forecasting models used by the Brazilian system operator, providing better information for generation scheduling.

Parameter estimation of synchronous machines considering field voltage variation during the sudden short-circuit test

V.A.D. de Faria, J.V. Bernardes, E.C. Bortoni, (2020). Parameter estimation of synchronous machines considering field voltage variation during the sudden short-circuit test, International Journal of Electrical Power & Energy Systems.

Sudden short-circuit tests typically assume constant field voltage, but actual excitation systems can violate that assumption. This study develops a method to recover synchronous-machine parameters even when substantial voltage variations occur. It also determines allowable combinations of variation magnitude and duration for a specified estimation error, and evaluates the approach using measurements from a 140 MVA machine.

Evaluation of manufacturers strategies to obtain high-efficient induction motors

E.C. Bortoni, J.V. Bernardes, P.V.V. da Silva, V.A.D. de Faria, P.A.V. Vieira, (2019). Evaluation of manufacturers strategies to obtain high-efficient induction motors, Sustainable Energy Technologies and Assessments.

This study uses manufacturers’ catalog data to estimate induction-motor circuit parameters and examine how design choices affect efficiency. A method separates short-circuit reactance into stator and rotor components using starting and breakdown torque, while accounting for changes in rotor behavior with slip. Comparisons across major European-market manufacturers reveal different approaches to reducing losses and meeting minimum efficiency requirements.

Cooperative Game Theory and Last Addition Method in the Allocation of Firm Energy Rights

V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, L.M.M. Lima, J.W.M. Lima, (2018). Cooperative Game Theory and Last Addition Method in the Allocation of Firm Energy Rights, Applied Energy.

Coordinated operation increases the dependable energy available from a hydropower system, raising the question of how to allocate that benefit among plants. This work combines cooperative game theory with the last-addition method to assign firm-energy trading rights. A faster mixed-integer formulation makes the analysis practical for a Brazilian system case, seeking allocations that reconcile generator incentives with regulatory objectives.
