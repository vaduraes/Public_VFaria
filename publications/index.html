Transfer learning and convolutional neural networks for solar photovoltaic systems detection in distributed power grids

Y.H. Chou†, V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, (2026). Transfer learning and convolutional neural networks for solar photovoltaic systems detection in distributed power grids, Renewable Energy (accepted, in editorial production).

Identifying rooftop solar in aerial images helps fill gaps in distributed-generation inventories. Using imagery from four California cities, this study compares segmentation architectures, transfer learning, and methods for reusing difficult training examples. Fully fine-tuning a pretrained model performed better than keeping its transferred layers fixed; reusing difficult examples did not consistently improve accuracy. Comparing predictions with the images also revealed inconsistent annotations, alongside missed panels and roof structures incorrectly identified as solar installations.

Journal of Infrastructure Systems journal cover

2026

Routing Optimization Framework for Exploring Time-Varying Urban Road Network Vulnerability under Floods

V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, A. Sankarasubramanian, R. Ranjithan, (2026). Routing Optimization Framework for Exploring Time-Varying Urban Road Network Vulnerability under Floods, Journal of Infrastructure Systems.

Flooding can isolate roads that remain dry by cutting their connections to the wider network. Stormwater simulations and time-dependent routing for 20,000 origin–destination pairs reveal these effects in Wilmington, North Carolina, during Hurricanes Florence, Dorian, and Matthew. Translating inundation into road closures and speed reductions exposes indirect neighborhood isolation and changes in which road connections are critical. Storm-specific information substantially improves route feasibility in the simulations, while the FEMA-based comparison underestimates disruption for all three events.

Fused Portfolio Optimization for Harnessing Marine Renewable Energy Resources

M. Maceda, R. Miller†, V.A.D. de Faria, M. Bryant, C. Vermillion, A.R. de Queiroz, (2026). Fused Portfolio Optimization for Harnessing Marine Renewable Energy Resources, Energy.

Offshore device design, site selection, and transmission capacity jointly determine how much electricity reaches shore. A spatial portfolio model combines optimized underwater-kite designs with wind-turbine options to maximize electricity delivered to shore under a cost limit. North Carolina case studies show how complementary wind and ocean-current resources can improve transmission utilization. Accounting for curtailment and testing cost assumptions helps connect device design choices with investment outcomes.

Journal of Cleaner Production journal cover

2025

Optimizing co-located offshore wave and wind energy systems: Feasibility, design, and portfolios assessment

N. Jamaleddin, V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, M. Gabr, (2025). Optimizing co-located offshore wave and wind energy systems: Feasibility, design, and portfolios assessment, Journal of Cleaner Production.

Sharing offshore infrastructure can reduce some costs while introducing engineering tradeoffs. This study combines mooring simulations under extreme conditions with portfolio optimization for wind turbines and Pelamis and RM3 wave devices off North Carolina. Shared anchors can reduce infrastructure requirements while increasing line lengths and spacing; transmission limits and curtailment also affect the preferred deployment. At the study’s baseline costs, additional wind can be more economical than adding wave devices, while projected reductions in wave costs improve the case for co-location.

Modeling energy storage in long-term capacity expansion energy planning: an analysis of the Italian system

M. Nicoli, V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, L. Savoldi, (2024). Modeling energy storage in long-term capacity expansion energy planning: an analysis of the Italian system, Journal of Energy Storage.

Storage needs depend on how planning models represent daily renewable generation and demand. The study updates TEMOA-Italy’s storage representation and daily generation and demand inputs, then compares time resolutions and technology choices through 2050. In the examined decarbonization scenarios with new electricity storage and no nuclear generation, 8–13% of electricity passes through storage. Finer time resolution changes storage utilization and computational effort, while alternative technology assumptions can substantially change the preferred investment mix.

Applied Ocean Research journal cover

Bayesian modeling and mechanical simulations for fragility curve estimation of the mooring system of marine hydrokinetic devices

V.A.D. de Faria, N. Jamaleddin, A.R. de Queiroz, M. Gabr, (2024). Bayesian modeling and mechanical simulations for fragility curve estimation of the mooring system of marine hydrokinetic devices, Applied Ocean Research.

Rapid changes in ocean currents can impose large transient loads on submerged turbines’ moorings. Bayesian analysis of current and wind measurements off North Carolina, combined with ANSYS AQWA simulations, estimates the probability of exceeding mooring operating limits at different wind speeds. At 40 m/s, modeled probabilities are about 12–14% when current-speed changes are included, compared with roughly 2–3% when their transient effects are omitted. The resulting fragility curves quantify how environmental uncertainty affects the studied mooring system’s vulnerability.

Scenario Generation and Risk-averse Stochastic Portfolio Optimization Applied to Offshore Renewable Energy Technologies

V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, J.F. DeCarolis, (2023). Scenario Generation and Risk-averse Stochastic Portfolio Optimization Applied to Offshore Renewable Energy Technologies, Energy.

A renewable portfolio with low variability can still perform poorly on days with little generation. A generative adversarial network creates daily wind, wave, and ocean-current scenarios that capture spatial relationships and correlations between resources across more than 500 offshore locations. A risk-averse portfolio model then maximizes average generation in the worst 5% of conditions under an electricity-cost limit. In the tested cases, targeting these low-generation conditions provides better protection than minimizing variance alone.

An Overview of Short-Term Load Forecasting for Electricity Systems Operational Planning: Machine Learning Methods and the Brazilian Experience

G. Aquila, L.B.S. Morais, V.A.D. de Faria, J.W.M. Lima, L.M.M. Lima, A.R. de Queiroz, (2023). An Overview of Short-Term Load Forecasting for Electricity Systems Operational Planning: Machine Learning Methods and the Brazilian Experience, Energies.

Short-term demand forecasts account for changing weather and consumer behavior to support generation scheduling and electricity pricing. This review examines statistical and machine learning methods, including choices about inputs, similar-day grouping, weather stations, hierarchical forecasts, and net demand after renewable generation. It relates these methods to Brazil’s operational planning, including DESSEM, and identifies opportunities to improve forecasts as distributed solar and other variable resources change the demand seen by the grid.

Short-term load forecasting using neural networks and global climate models: An application to a large-scale electrical power system

L.B.S. Morais, G. Aquila, V.A.D. de Faria, L.M.M. Lima, J.W.M. Lima, A.R. de Queiroz, (2023). Short-term load forecasting using neural networks and global climate models: An application to a large-scale electrical power system, Applied Energy.

Feedforward and recurrent neural networks forecast next-day electricity demand in half-hour intervals for Brazil’s Central-Southeast market, with statistical tests used to compare accuracy. With system-operator temperature inputs, a bidirectional LSTM achieved 1.18% mean absolute percentage error over the three-month test, compared with 2.01% for the operator’s forecasts. A separate full-year 2019 experiment found NOAA ensemble temperature forecasts useful as alternative inputs. Operator load forecasts were unavailable for that period, so the full-year experiment has no direct operator benchmark.

Optimizing Offshore Renewable Portfolios Under Resource Variability

V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, J.F. DeCarolis, (2022). Optimizing Offshore Renewable Portfolios Under Resource Variability, Applied Energy.

Offshore sites with the lowest electricity costs do not necessarily produce the steadiest combined output. A mean–variance portfolio model evaluates more than 5,000 candidate wind, wave, and ocean-current locations off North Carolina, balancing generation variability against a cost limit. Evaluating selected portfolios in TEMOA estimates their value to the wider electricity system. Diversifying resources smooths generation, but substantial cost reductions remain necessary for deployment under the study’s technology and planning assumptions.

An assessment of multi-layer perceptron networks for streamflow forecasting in large-scale interconnected hydrosystems

V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, L.M.M. Lima, J.W.M. Lima, B.C. Silva, (2022). An assessment of multi-layer perceptron networks for streamflow forecasting in large-scale interconnected hydrosystems, International Journal of Environmental Science and Technology.

Neural networks forecast natural streamflow up to 14 days ahead for 55 hydropower plants in Brazil’s Paraná Basin. The study introduces a procedure for selecting inputs across the river network and compares training methods, loss functions, and regularization. Percentage errors were lower than the system operator’s for every plant and year in 2014–2016; performance on Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency, which is more sensitive to flow peaks, varied by year. A separate five-plant experiment found no consistent benefit from adding historical rainfall.

Parameter estimation of synchronous machines considering field voltage variation during the sudden short-circuit test

V.A.D. de Faria, J.V. Bernardes, E.C. Bortoni, (2020). Parameter estimation of synchronous machines considering field voltage variation during the sudden short-circuit test, International Journal of Electrical Power & Energy Systems.

Sudden short-circuit tests help identify generator parameters, but changes in field voltage can distort estimates that assume constant excitation. Incorporating the voltage history into nonlinear parameter estimation substantially reduces errors in simulations of a 140 MVA machine using manufacturer data. A second algorithm identifies voltage-drop magnitudes and durations for which the conventional method stays within a chosen error tolerance. The analysis also identifies limitations for small machines with fast transients.

Renewable Energy journal cover

Hydropower Revenues under the Threat of Climate Change in Brazil

A.R. de Queiroz, V.A.D. de Faria, L.M.M. Lima, J.W.M. Lima, (2019). Hydropower Revenues under the Threat of Climate Change in Brazil, Renewable Energy.

Climate-driven changes in river flows can affect both hydropower generation and the energy available to support sales contracts. The analysis combines regional climate projections, hydrological modeling, and optimization to estimate assured-energy allocations for individual Brazilian hydropower plants. The modeled scenarios show pronounced geographic differences: many southern plants gain assured energy while northern and northeastern plants lose it, with planned run-of-river developments particularly exposed. An illustrative revenue assessment translates these physical changes into financial effects using a fixed reference electricity value.

Evaluation of manufacturers strategies to obtain high-efficient induction motors

E.C. Bortoni, J.V. Bernardes, P.V.V. da Silva, V.A.D. de Faria, P.A.V. Vieira, (2019). Evaluation of manufacturers strategies to obtain high-efficient induction motors, Sustainable Energy Technologies and Assessments.

Manufacturers improve induction-motor efficiency through different combinations of reduced electrical and mechanical losses. An algebraic method estimates equivalent-circuit parameters from catalog data, separating stator and rotor reactance while accounting for changes in rotor behavior with slip. Comparisons of dozens of motors from four manufacturers reveal different paths to higher efficiency classes. The method makes it possible to interpret these differences without proprietary design information or an optimization solver.

Cooperative Game Theory and Last Addition Method in the Allocation of Firm Energy Rights

V.A.D. de Faria, A.R. de Queiroz, L.M.M. Lima, J.W.M. Lima, (2018). Cooperative Game Theory and Last Addition Method in the Allocation of Firm Energy Rights, Applied Energy.

Coordinated hydropower operation creates benefits that must be shared among plant owners. The algorithm combines allocations based on each plant’s incremental contribution with cooperative game constraints that limit incentives for groups of plants to leave the system. A reformulated optimization model makes the approach practical for a 170-plant Brazilian case, requiring 21 coalition constraints to reach the chosen tolerance. The results connect investment incentives with stable allocations and show how the reservoir-balance formulation affects computational performance.

Multidimensional uncertainty assessment in a capacity expansion model for the U.S. power system

V.A.D. de Faria, B. Sergi, P.R. Brown, (2026). Multidimensional uncertainty assessment in a capacity expansion model for the U.S. power system, 19th International Conference on Probabilistic Methods Applied to Power Systems (PMAPS), Salt Lake City, UT, USA.

A single forecast can conceal substantial uncertainty in electricity investment decisions. Using 1,137 ReEDS simulations, this study examines how demand, technology costs, gas prices, renewable siting, and policy assumptions affect U.S. generation and transmission investment through 2050. Sampling choices change the spread of outcomes more than their central values, while the tested federal policy alternatives can produce larger shifts in system design. Interactions between inputs also matter, including the combined influence of nuclear construction costs and gas prices.

Accepted; in editorial production. Forthcoming in IEEE Xplore.

How the U.S. Power Grid Kept the Lights on in Summer 2024

P. Denholm, V.A.D. de Faria, J. Frost, (2024). How the U.S. Power Grid Kept the Lights on in Summer 2024, National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL).

Observed operating data show how the U.S. power grid met high summer demand in 2024, with a detailed ERCOT case and comparisons across New England, California, and MISO. Solar supplied substantial afternoon generation in several regions, shifted net-demand peaks toward evening, and shortened the periods that storage needed to cover. Thermal generation and other resources continued to contribute. The analysis shows why reliability planning must account for changing stress periods and the complementary roles of different technologies.
