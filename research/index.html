Research

Models and methods for planning, operations, and uncertainty.

Energy planning and operations

I study how energy systems should be built and operated as technologies, demand, and resource availability change. My work includes capacity expansion, production cost modeling, and probabilistic resource adequacy assessment.

ReEDS uncertainty assessment. Monte Carlo workflows to study how uncertain technology costs and fuel prices affect generation investments.

Operations at Duke Energy. Optimization methods that connect dispatch decisions with natural gas availability, fuel inventories, delivery constraints, and changing market conditions.

Storage in capacity expansion. Model development to capture operational features of storage in energy planning.

Decision making under uncertainty

I develop stochastic optimization and statistical models to represent uncertainty in resource availability, infrastructure damage, and future energy system conditions.

Capacity expansion under hurricane risk. A multistage stochastic extension of TEMOA that represents hurricane damage and infrastructure vulnerability in electricity investment decisions.

Marine energy fragility. Bayesian modeling and mechanical simulations to estimate mooring system failure probabilities under extreme conditions.

Urban flood vulnerability. Routing models that account for evolving flood conditions and their effect on road accessibility.

Energy portfolios and computational methods

I work on methods that connect technology selection, location, engineering design, and uncertainty to the economics of energy investments.

Marine energy portfolios. Optimization models for joint decisions about technologies, sites, and device sizes.

Renewable generation scenarios. Generative models that represent variability and correlations in wind, wave, and ocean current resources.

Forecasting and data analysis. Neural network models for hydropower inflows and collaborative work on photovoltaic detection.
