Multidimensional Uncertainty Assessment in a Capacity Expansion Model for the U.S. Power System

PMAPS 2026 · Salt Lake City, Utah

Talk · September 22, 2026

This talk examines how uncertain demand, technology costs, fuel prices, and renewable resources affect U.S. electricity investment through 2050. Using ReEDS, it varies several inputs together to reveal outcomes that changing one assumption at a time can miss. Comparisons of sampling methods and policy scenarios show how uncertainty affects the range of investment outcomes and help identify the assumptions with the greatest influence on costs, prices, and emissions.

Victor A. D. de Faria, Brian Sergi, and Patrick R. Brown

Presented by: Patrick R. Brown

Exploring the Feasibility of Co-located Marine Renewable Energy Systems: A Mooring System and Economic Analysis in the North Carolina Coast

UMERC+METS 2024 · Duluth, Minnesota

Poster · August 7, 2024

Combining wave devices with offshore wind farms could make better use of shared infrastructure. This poster examines mooring arrangements and uses portfolio optimization to select devices, sites, and transmission infrastructure off North Carolina. The results show how wave generation can use transmission capacity left available by wind, while device spacing, mooring costs, and projected reductions in wave technology costs determine which combinations are attractive.

Neda Jamaleddin, Victor A. D. de Faria, Anderson R. de Queiroz, and Mohammed Gabr

Presented by: Neda Jamaleddin

Predictive and Prescriptive Analytics Applied to Energy Systems

Ph.D. Dissertation Defense · North Carolina State University

Dissertation defense · June 19, 2024

My dissertation connects forecasting and uncertainty modeling with decisions about energy investment. The defense brings together offshore renewable portfolio optimization, generative neural networks for resource scenarios, and streamflow forecasting for hydropower. It also examines how policy, technology costs, and hurricane damage affect electricity planning in North Carolina. Together, these studies show how predictive models and mathematical optimization can inform energy decisions under uncertain conditions.

Victor A. D. de Faria

Energy Systems Capacity Expansion Planning Under the Risk of Hurricane Damage

NC State Graduate Student Research Symposium · Raleigh, North Carolina

Poster · April 3, 2024

This poster presents an electricity planning model that accounts for hurricane damage to existing and future infrastructure in North Carolina. Wind estimates and engineering vulnerability models translate storm intensity into reductions in available generating capacity. The model then compares investment choices across possible hurricane scenarios, technology costs, and emissions targets, providing a framework for assessing how exposure to damage affects the cost of meeting future demand.

Energy Systems Capacity Expansion Planning Under the Risk of Hurricane Damage

INFORMS Annual Meeting · Phoenix, Arizona

Talk · October 15, 2023

This talk introduces an extension of TEMOA that incorporates hurricane damage into electricity investment planning. Wind estimates and technology vulnerability curves describe how storms could reduce generating capacity in different parts of North Carolina. The model evaluates investment decisions across a sequence of possible hurricane scenarios and compares them with a plan that excludes hurricane damage, demonstrating a way to connect physical infrastructure risk with long-term system costs.

Victor A. D. de Faria, Matteo Nicoli, and Anderson R. de Queiroz

Modeling Energy Storage in Capacity Expansion Models: An Application to TEMOA-Italy

INFORMS Annual Meeting · Phoenix, Arizona

Presentation · October 15, 2023

This presentation examines the role of storage in pathways to decarbonize Italy’s energy system through 2050. Using TEMOA-Italy, it compares technology options and different levels of detail in daily electricity demand and generation. In the modeled scenarios where storage supports full power-sector decarbonization, roughly 10% of electricity passes through storage by 2050. The results show how daily operating patterns affect storage use and why additional modeling detail must be weighed against computation time.

Matteo Nicoli, Victor A. D. de Faria, Anderson R. de Queiroz, and Laura Savoldi

Presented by: Matteo Nicoli

Mooring System Analysis and Fragility Curve Estimation: The Economic Impact of Low Probability High Impact Events on Ocean Current Devices

NCROEP 12th Annual Symposium

Talk · March 20, 2023

This talk examines how extreme ocean currents affect the moorings that hold underwater turbines in place. Environmental data and ANSYS AQWA simulations are used to compare mooring configurations, estimate loads, and assess vulnerability under severe conditions. The framework connects engineering design with estimates of damage risk, helping evaluate the economic implications of rare but damaging events for ocean-current energy projects.

Assessing the Risk of Hurricane Damage to Marine Hydrokinetic Devices

UMERC+METS 2022 · Portland, Oregon

Poster · September 14, 2022

This poster presents a method for estimating hurricane damage risk to the moorings that hold marine energy devices in place. Bayesian analysis and ANSYS AQWA mechanical simulations link hurricane wind speeds to estimates of mooring vulnerability. The resulting fragility curves describe how damage probability changes with storm intensity, providing information for device design and investment risk assessment.

Victor A. D. de Faria, Neda Jamaleddin, Anderson R. de Queiroz, Mohammed Gabr, and Mike Muglia

Analyzing Investments in NC Offshore Renewable Energy Under Uncertainty About Resource Availability and Hurricane Damage

NCROEP Symposium · Virtual

Poster · May 25, 2021

This poster explores how uncertainty in resource availability and hurricane damage can inform offshore renewable energy investment in North Carolina. Stochastic programming is used to evaluate portfolios of wind, wave, and ocean-current technologies and to represent hurricane scenarios in electricity capacity planning. Considering multiple possible futures helps assess how generation variability and infrastructure damage could influence the choice of offshore projects and the wider energy system’s development.

Victor A. D. de Faria, Anderson R. de Queiroz, and Joseph F. DeCarolis

Presented by: Victor A. D. de Faria
