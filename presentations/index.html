Multidimensional Uncertainty Assessment in a Capacity Expansion Model for the U.S. Power System

PMAPS 2026 · Salt Lake City, Utah

Talk · September 22, 2026

Monte Carlo sampling and global sensitivity analysis examine how uncertain inputs affect U.S. power-system investments in ReEDS. Technology costs, fuel prices, electricity demand, and renewable resources vary together, with tests of different probability distributions and regional correlations. The resulting range of capacity additions reveals which assumptions most strongly influence system costs, electricity prices, and emissions.

Victor A. D. de Faria, Brian Sergi, and Patrick R. Brown

Presented by: Patrick R. Brown

Exploring the Feasibility of Co-located Marine Renewable Energy Systems: A Mooring System and Economic Analysis in the North Carolina Coast

UMERC+METS 2024 · Duluth, Minnesota

Poster · August 7, 2024

A portfolio optimization model evaluates combined offshore wind and wave generation along the North Carolina coast. It selects device locations and transmission infrastructure to maximize electricity delivered to shore within a cost limit. Wave devices can use spare transmission capacity alongside wind generation, with the economic value of co-location depending on future wave-technology costs.

Neda Jamaleddin, Victor A. D. de Faria, Anderson R. de Queiroz, and Mohammed Gabr

Predictive and Prescriptive Analytics Applied to Energy Systems

Ph.D. Dissertation Defense · North Carolina State University

Dissertation defense · June 19, 2024

My dissertation combines predictive analytics and mathematical optimization for energy-system planning. The defense covers offshore renewable portfolio design, scenario generation using generative neural networks, risk-averse stochastic optimization, and neural-network streamflow forecasting. A North Carolina capacity expansion case study examines how policies, technology cost trajectories, and hurricane risks can influence investment decisions.

Energy Systems Capacity Expansion Planning Under the Risk of Hurricane Damage

INFORMS Annual Meeting · Phoenix, Arizona

Talk · October 15, 2023

FEMA HAZUS wind estimates and technology-specific fragility curves translate hurricane conditions into regional infrastructure damage in North Carolina. A multistage stochastic TEMOA model uses these estimates to evaluate generation investments across possible hurricane scenarios. Comparing planning decisions and system costs with a deterministic case shows how accounting for hurricane risk affects capacity expansion.

Victor A. D. de Faria, Matteo Nicoli, and Anderson R. de Queiroz

Modeling Energy Storage in Capacity Expansion Models: An Application to TEMOA-Italy

INFORMS Annual Meeting · Phoenix, Arizona

Presentation · October 15, 2023

This presentation examines how energy storage and temporal resolution affect capacity expansion planning using the TEMOA model of Italy’s energy system. It compares different time resolutions and technology options to assess storage needs and pathways to decarbonization through 2050. The results suggest that electricity storage can help fully decarbonize the power sector, with roughly 10% of electricity passing through storage by 2050 in the relevant scenarios. Finer time resolution changes storage utilization and increases computational effort, highlighting the importance of representing daily renewable generation and demand patterns in long-term planning.

Matteo Nicoli, Victor A. D. de Faria, Anderson R. de Queiroz, and Laura Savoldi

Presented by: Matteo Nicoli

Assessing the Risk of Hurricane Damage to Marine Hydrokinetic Devices

UMERC+METS 2022 · Portland, Oregon

Poster · September 14, 2022

This work uses Bayesian analysis and mechanical model simulations through the Ansys-AQWA software to construct fragility curve estimates for marine hydrokinetic devices, more specifically, their mooring system. The fragility curves proposed here associate hurricane speed levels with the risk of damage to the equipment and could be used to better understand the susceptibility of these devices to damage from hurricanes.

Victor A. D. de Faria, Neda Jamaleddin, Anderson R. de Queiroz, Mohammed Gabr, and Mike Muglia

Analyzing Investments in NC Offshore Renewable Energy Under Uncertainty About Resource Availability and Hurricane Damage

NCROEP Symposium · Virtual

Poster · May 25, 2021

This work investigates the use of stochastic programming in the portfolio optimization of offshore wind, wave and ocean current technology. We also use the stochastic programming technique in the context of a capacity expansion model, to help understand how the incorporation of hurricane damage scenarios can influence how the North Carolina energy system may evolve in the future and how the offshore energy technologies may be affected by that.
